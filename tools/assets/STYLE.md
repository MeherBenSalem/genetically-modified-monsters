# Spore & Stem: art style guide

Every texture, model and animation is **generated by code** in `tools/assets/ss/` so it stays consistent,
reviewable and re-runnable. The bar is production quality: every sprite must look like it belongs next to vanilla
Minecraft, read clearly at 1× in a hotbar slot, and be distinct from its neighbours. No placeholders, no flat
single-colour blobs, no noise-filled squares, never a pixel copied from vanilla or another mod (vanilla may be
*looked at* for scale, brightness and outline conventions).

## Identity

**Clean fungus.** Natural cap colours, crisp silhouettes, soft spores, and one bioluminescent accent: the teal
`palette.GLOW`, shared by Glowcaps, Lumen Spores, Glowmoths, veil bulbs and the Myceliarch's gills. Eerie and
beautiful, never gross: no gore, no slime piles, no body horror. Colours come from `ss/palette.py`; add a ramp there
rather than inventing hex values inline.

## Canvas and format

- Items and block faces: **16×16 RGBA PNG**. Mob effect icons: 18×18. Particles: 8×8 frames. Logo: 128×128 (drawn at
  native pixel scale, no smoothing). Entity textures: 64×64 or 128×128, armor layers 64×32.
- Items and cutout blocks use alpha 0 or 255 only (cutout drops anything in between). Translucency only where a
  translucent render type is used.

## Light and shading

- One light from the **top-left**. Highlights top-left, core shadow bottom-right. Shade by form, not by pillow
  (don't darken every edge equally).
- Ramps are hue-shifted: shadows drift toward blue/violet, highlights toward warm yellow (`core.shade`,
  `core.ramp`, or the hand-tuned ramps in `palette.py`). 3-5 tones per material.
- Caps are glossy: one or two near-white specular pixels on the lit shoulder. Stems, gills and wood are matte.
- Glowing parts (Glowcap caps and gills, bulbs, ember tips, eyes) use the brightest two tones of their ramp and are
  repeated in the emissive layer for creatures.

## Items

- **1px outline** in a dark, saturated, hue-shifted version of the neighbouring colour (`core` outline helpers), not
  pure black; darkest bottom-right.
- 12-14 px of the canvas, centred, ≥1 px margin. Long things (knife, skewer, Bloomreaver) run diagonally bottom-left
  to top-right like vanilla tools.
- Silhouette first: each item must read by silhouette alone. Siblings share a silhouette and differ by ramp.
- Containers match vanilla: soups in the vanilla-proportioned wooden bowl, drinks in the vanilla glass-bottle
  silhouette.

## Blocks

- **Placed mushrooms are real 3D models** (stem + layered cap boxes, ≤ 12 elements), never flat crosses. Their
  inventory icons are hand-drawn 2D sprites. Randomly rotated variants in the blockstate (y 0/90/180/270).
- Wood sets read like their species: Glowcap wood is cool blue-grey with faint teal grain, Bouncecap wood rosy tan,
  Inkcap wood deep charcoal-violet. Planks show 4 boards with grain and nail-free ends, like vanilla planks. Stems are
  fibrous (vertical hyphae streaks), with a ring-structured top; hyphae use the stem side on all faces.
- Caps tile seamlessly on all faces; the inside texture shows gills/pores.
- Doors and trapdoors have a clear frame and a fungal motif (cap silhouette, gill slits). Leave cut-out windows only
  where the motif needs them.
- Every model sets `textures.particle`, a full `display` block where it is not inherited, and
  `"render_type": "minecraft:cutout"` whenever any texture has transparent pixels (NeoForge reads it; Fabric uses
  `ModBlocks.CUTOUT`).

## Creatures

- Geometry, UVs, textures and animations come from one source per creature in `ss/entities/`. Java model classes
  and animation definitions are generated from it, never hand-edited.
- Faces read at a distance: eyes 1-2 px with a glint, contrasted against the cap shadow.
- Animations use Catmull-Rom keyframes with anticipation and follow-through: caps lag behind bodies, arms overshoot
  then settle. Idle loops never stand perfectly still (breathing, cap sway, blinking).

## Review loop

Write contact sheets and 3D renders to `tools/assets/build/previews/` (git-ignored) and look at them at scale before
calling anything done. Then look at it in game (`tools/qa/`), in daylight and in the dark.

## Other Minecraft versions (ports)

Every module generates for the reference workspace, 1.21.1. `build.py` then derives each other workspace's
`common/src/generated/resources` from that tree with a port module in `ss/ports/` (`v1_20_1.py` for 1.20.1, listed
in `PORTS`). A port renames data folders, rewrites recipe, loot, advancement and tag formats, swaps loader-specific
files (NeoForge biome modifiers become Forge's, model `neoforge_data` becomes `forge_data`, NeoForge data maps are
dropped) and maps conventional tags. Each handler is strict: an unknown key, type or trigger stops the build rather
than being copied through. The result is then checked against the vanilla client and registry reports of that
version, downloaded once into `build/vanilla-<version>/`. Lossy conversions are listed in `build/port-<version>.txt`.
The creature geometry and animation Java is written into every workspace.

- Never generate version-specific output in a module. Generate the 1.21.1 form and convert it in the port.
- `python3 tools/assets/check.py --workspace 1.20.1` checks a port against its own registry dump (from a 1.20.1
  dev run) and its vanilla reference.
- To add a version, copy the closest port to `ss/ports/v<version>.py`, adjust its tables and handlers, add it to
  `PORTS`, and run `build.py` until it converts and validates cleanly.
